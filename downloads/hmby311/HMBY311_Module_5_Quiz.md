## Part I: Question Bank

### Question 1
**What biological discipline is defined as the scientific study of heredity in general and of genes in particular?**

- **a.)** Cytology
- **b.)** Genomics
- **c.)** Genetics
- **d.)** Histology

> **Answer:** **C** — *Genetics*
> **Topic:** `Genetics Fundamentals`
> **Explanation:** Genetics is the study of heredity in general and of genes in particular, forming a central pillar of biology.

### Question 2
**Gregor Mendel referred to the inherited units passed onto descendants as 'factors', which are now scientifically known as what?**

- **a.)** Enzymes
- **b.)** Genes
- **c.)** Histones
- **d.)** Chromatids

> **Answer:** **B** — *Genes*
> **Topic:** `Genetics Fundamentals`
> **Explanation:** Inheritance of traits is determined by factors passed to descendants, now designated as genes.

### Question 3
**What term describes the alternative or different forms of the same gene located at the same locus on homologous chromosomes?**

- **a.)** Histones
- **b.)** Codons
- **c.)** Alleles
- **d.)** Autosomes

> **Answer:** **C** — *Alleles*
> **Topic:** `Alleles & Zygosity`
> **Explanation:** Alleles are different forms of the same gene.

### Question 4
**An individual that possesses a pair of identical alleles for a particular gene (such as AA or aa) is described as what?**

- **a.)** Homozygous
- **b.)** Hemizygous
- **c.)** Heterozygous
- **d.)** Polygenic

> **Answer:** **A** — *Homozygous*
> **Topic:** `Alleles & Zygosity`
> **Explanation:** An individual with a pair of the same alleles is identified as homozygous.

### Question 5
**An individual that possesses two different alleles for a particular gene (such as Aa) is described as what?**

- **a.)** Monohybrid
- **b.)** Homozygous
- **c.)** Heterozygous
- **d.)** Aneuploid

> **Answer:** **C** — *Heterozygous*
> **Topic:** `Alleles & Zygosity`
> **Explanation:** An individual with different alleles for a gene is referred to as heterozygous.

### Question 6
**In a heterozygous condition, what term describes the allele that masks the other allele and is phenotypically expressed?**

- **a.)** Incomplete
- **b.)** Codominant
- **c.)** Dominant
- **d.)** Recessive

> **Answer:** **C** — *Dominant*
> **Topic:** `Alleles & Zygosity`
> **Explanation:** In heterozygous individuals, the allele that is expressed is described as being dominant.

### Question 7
**In a heterozygous condition, what term describes the allele whose phenotypic expression is masked or suppressed?**

- **a.)** Carrier
- **b.)** Dominant
- **c.)** Codominant
- **d.)** Recessive

> **Answer:** **D** — *Recessive*
> **Topic:** `Alleles & Zygosity`
> **Explanation:** The allele that is not expressed (masked) in a heterozygote is called recessive.

### Question 8
**At the molecular level, what does a recessive allele typically produce compared to a dominant allele?**

- **a.)** A nonfunctional protein or no protein at all
- **b.)** A toxic protein
- **c.)** An identical duplicate protein
- **d.)** A structural lipid

> **Answer:** **A** — *A nonfunctional protein or no protein at all*
> **Topic:** `Alleles & Zygosity`
> **Explanation:** The dominant allele usually produces a functional protein, while the recessive allele produces either a nonfunctional protein or no protein at all.

### Question 9
**Which Mendelian law states that allele pairs separate during gamete formation so that each gamete carries only one allele for each gene?**

- **a.)** Law of Polygenic Inheritance
- **b.)** Law of Segregation
- **c.)** Law of Independent Assortment
- **d.)** Law of Dominance

> **Answer:** **B** — *Law of Segregation*
> **Topic:** `Mendelian Principles`
> **Explanation:** The Law of Segregation states that allele pairs separate during gamete formation, with half receiving one allele and half the other.

### Question 10
**Which Mendelian law states that each pair of alleles located on one kind of chromosome separates into gametes independently of alleles on different chromosomes?**

- **a.)** Law of Continuous Variation
- **b.)** Law of Segregation
- **c.)** Law of Independent Assortment
- **d.)** Law of Dominance

> **Answer:** **C** — *Law of Independent Assortment*
> **Topic:** `Mendelian Principles`
> **Explanation:** The Law of Independent Assortment dictates that genes on different chromosomes segregate independently into gametes.

### Question 11
**What genetic term refers to the precise genetic makeup or combination of alleles present within an individual?**

- **a.)** Phenotype
- **b.)** Karyotype
- **c.)** Genotype
- **d.)** Pedigree

> **Answer:** **C** — *Genotype*
> **Topic:** `Genotype vs Phenotype`
> **Explanation:** The genotype refers to the specific alleles present and combined in an individual (e.g., AA, Aa, aa).

### Question 12
**What term describes the observable physical, physiological, or biochemical traits expressed by an individual?**

- **a.)** Allele frequency
- **b.)** Genotype
- **c.)** Genome
- **d.)** Phenotype

> **Answer:** **D** — *Phenotype*
> **Topic:** `Genotype vs Phenotype`
> **Explanation:** The phenotype refers to the observable physical or physiological traits expressed in the individual.

### Question 13
**What diagrammatic tool is used in genetics to calculate and predict the probabilities of offspring genotypes and phenotypes from parental gametes?**

- **a.)** Chomsky grid
- **b.)** Punnett square
- **c.)** Pedigree chart
- **d.)** Karyotype diagram

> **Answer:** **B** — *Punnett square*
> **Topic:** `Genetic Crosses`
> **Explanation:** A Punnett square is used to determine the probability that each gamete cross will occur.

### Question 14
**If a homozygous dominant individual (AA) crosses with a homozygous recessive individual (aa), what will be the genotype of all their offspring?**

- **a.)** 25% AA, 75% aa
- **b.)** 50% AA, 50% aa
- **c.)** 100% Heterozygous (Aa)
- **d.)** 100% Homozygous dominant (AA)

> **Answer:** **C** — *100% Heterozygous (Aa)*
> **Topic:** `Genetic Crosses`
> **Explanation:** Crossing AA x aa yields 100% heterozygous (Aa) offspring expressing the dominant phenotype.

### Question 15
**When two heterozygous individuals (Aa x Aa) mate, what is the expected genotypic ratio among their offspring?**

- **a.)** 100% Aa
- **b.)** 25% AA, 50% Aa, 25% aa
- **c.)** 50% AA, 50% aa
- **d.)** 75% Aa, 25% aa

> **Answer:** **B** — *25% AA, 50% Aa, 25% aa*
> **Topic:** `Genetic Crosses`
> **Explanation:** An Aa x Aa cross produces 25% homozygous dominant (AA), 50% heterozygous (Aa), and 25% homozygous recessive (aa).

### Question 16
**When two heterozygous individuals (Aa x Aa) mate, what is the expected phenotypic ratio for a trait following complete dominance?**

- **a.)** 100% dominant
- **b.)** 75% dominant, 25% recessive
- **c.)** 50% dominant, 50% recessive
- **d.)** 25% dominant, 75% recessive

> **Answer:** **B** — *75% dominant, 25% recessive*
> **Topic:** `Genetic Crosses`
> **Explanation:** Because AA and Aa both show the dominant phenotype, 75% exhibit the dominant trait and 25% show the recessive trait.

### Question 17
**What term is given to a heterozygous individual who displays a normal/dominant phenotype but carries a recessive allele for a genetic disorder?**

- **a.)** Vector

- **c.)** Mutant
- **d.)** Carrier

> **Answer:** **D** — *Carrier*
> **Topic:** `Genetic Crosses`
> **Explanation:** A heterozygous individual that shows the dominant phenotype while carrying a recessive allele is called a carrier.

### Question 19
**What type of non-Mendelian inheritance occurs when both alleles are simultaneously and clearly apparent in the heterozygous phenotype?**

- **a.)** Incomplete dominance
- **b.)** Polygenic inheritance
- **c.)** Codominance
- **d.)** Complete dominance

> **Answer:** **C** — *Codominance*
> **Topic:** `Non-Mendelian Inheritance`
> **Explanation:** Codominance takes place when both alleles are fully and distinctly expressed in the phenotype (e.g., AB blood type).

### Question 20
**What pattern of inheritance occurs when a heterozygous phenotype is intermediate between the two homozygous phenotypes (e.g., wavy hair from curly and straight)?**

- **a.)** Incomplete dominance
- **b.)** Sex-linkage
- **c.)** Pleiotropy
- **d.)** Codominance

> **Answer:** **A** — *Incomplete dominance*
> **Topic:** `Non-Mendelian Inheritance`
> **Explanation:** Incomplete dominance occurs when expression in a heterozygote is intermediate between homozygous dominant and homozygous recessive.

### Question 21
**What genetic scenario occurs when there are three or more distinct alleles for a single gene existing within a population, as exemplified by the ABO blood group system?**

- **a.)** Polygenic inheritance
- **b.)** Multiple alleles
- **c.)** Gene duplication
- **d.)** Epistasis

> **Answer:** **B** — *Multiple alleles*
> **Topic:** `Non-Mendelian Inheritance`
> **Explanation:** Multiple alleles refers to the condition where three or more alleles exist for a particular gene in a population, such as ABO blood types (IA, IB, i).

### Question 22
**What form of inheritance occurs when two or more distinct genes govern the expression of a single continuous phenotypic trait such as height, skin color, or eye color?**

- **a.)** Incomplete dominance
- **b.)** Monogenic inheritance
- **c.)** Multiple alleles
- **d.)** Polygenic inheritance

> **Answer:** **D** — *Polygenic inheritance*
> **Topic:** `Non-Mendelian Inheritance`
> **Explanation:** Polygenic inheritance occurs when more than one gene controls the trait. Examples include human height, skin pigmentation, and eye color.

### Question 40
