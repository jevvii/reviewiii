## Part I: Question Bank

### Question 1
**The condensed form of DNA in a cell that surrounds and wraps around spool-like proteins called histones is known as what structure?**

- **a.)** Centriole
- **b.)** Ribosome
- **c.)** Chromosome
- **d.)** Nucleolus

> **Answer:** **C** — *Chromosome*
> **Topic:** `Forms of Chromosomes`
> **Explanation:** A chromosome contains DNA in its condensed form wrapped around proteins called histones.

### Question 2
**What proteins serve as the molecular spools around which DNA coils and condenses into chromatin and chromosomes?**

- **a.)** Spindle fibers
- **b.)** Histones
- **c.)** Centrosomes
- **d.)** Ribosomes

> **Answer:** **B** — *Histones*
> **Topic:** `Forms of Chromosomes`
> **Explanation:** DNA wraps around proteins called histones to form compact chromatin and chromosomes, preventing breakage during cell division.

### Question 3
**A specific sequence of DNA that codes for a protein with a structural or functional role in the cell is defined as a what?**

- **a.)** Codon
- **b.)** Centromere
- **c.)** Gene
- **d.)** Histone

> **Answer:** **C** — *Gene*
> **Topic:** `Forms of Chromosomes`
> **Explanation:** A gene is a sequence of DNA arranged in a specific manner that codes for a protein with a structural or functional role in the cell.

### Question 4
**How many different kinds of chromosomes are present in human cells?**

- **a.)** 23 kinds
- **b.)** 22 kinds
- **c.)** 46 kinds
- **d.)** 2 kinds

> **Answer:** **A** — *23 kinds*
> **Topic:** `Forms of Chromosomes`
> **Explanation:** Each of the 23 different kinds of chromosomes in human cells contains a specific sequence of genes.

### Question 6
**What percentage of our modern human DNA is unique to modern humans and distinct from early human ancestors?**

- **a.)** 1%
- **b.)** 23%
- **c.)** 7%

> **Answer:** **C** — *7%*
> **Topic:** `Human Genome Project`
> **Explanation:** Out of the 99% of sequenced human genome, 7% of our DNA is unique to modern humans compared to early ancestors.

### Question 7
**All cells within the human body except for reproductive cells (eggs and sperm) are classified as what type of cells?**

- **a.)** Zygotes
- **b.)** Gametes
- **c.)** Sex cells
- **d.)** Somatic cells

> **Answer:** **D** — *Somatic cells*
> **Topic:** `Somatic Cells`
> **Explanation:** Somatic cells refer to all cells within the human body and are diploid (46 chromosomes) except eggs and sperm.

### Question 8
**What is the total diploid chromosome number present in normal human somatic cells?**

- **a.)** 46 chromosomes
- **b.)** 23 chromosomes
- **c.)** 92 chromosomes
- **d.)** 44 chromosomes

> **Answer:** **A** — *46 chromosomes*
> **Topic:** `Somatic Cells`
> **Explanation:** Somatic cells are diploid, containing 46 chromosomes (23 homologous pairs).

### Question 9
**What term describes human reproductive cells (eggs and sperm) that contain only one set of 23 chromosomes?**

- **a.)** Polyploid
- **b.)** Haploid
- **c.)** Diploid
- **d.)** Tetraploid

> **Answer:** **B** — *Haploid*
> **Topic:** `Gametes`
> **Explanation:** Eggs and sperm are haploid, meaning they contain only one set of 23 chromosomes.

### Question 10
**Chromosomes that occur in pairs and contain genes that code for or affect the same biological traits are called what?**

- **a.)** Heterozygous chromosomes
- **b.)** Analogous chromosomes
- **c.)** Homologous chromosomes
- **d.)** Sister chromatids

> **Answer:** **C** — *Homologous chromosomes*
> **Topic:** `Homologous Chromosomes`
> **Explanation:** Homologous chromosomes are pairs that contain genes coding for or affecting the same traits, one from each parent.

### Question 11
**How many homologous chromosome pairs are found in a normal human somatic cell?**

- **a.)** 46 pairs
- **b.)** 22 pairs
- **c.)** 23 pairs
- **d.)** 1 pair

> **Answer:** **C** — *23 pairs*
> **Topic:** `Homologous Chromosomes`
> **Explanation:** The diploid number of 46 chromosomes translates to 23 homologous pairs in each somatic cell.

### Question 12

> **Topic:** `Sex Chromosomes`

### Question 15
**The 22 pairs of human chromosomes that do not determine gender are called what?**

- **a.)** Chromatids

### Question 16
**Which type of nuclear division produces two genetically identical daughter cells with the same number of chromosomes as the parent cell for growth and tissue repair?**

- **a.)** Binary fission
- **b.)** Mitosis
- **c.)** Meiosis
- **d.)** Fertilization

> **Answer:** **B** — *Mitosis*
> **Topic:** `Types of Cell Division`
> **Explanation:** Mitosis creates daughter cells with the exact same number of chromosomes as the original cell, functioning in growth and tissue repair.

### Question 17
**Which type of nuclear division reduces the chromosome number by half and occurs specifically during the production of gametes?**

- **a.)** Cytokinesis
- **b.)** Interphase
- **c.)** Mitosis
- **d.)** Meiosis

> **Answer:** **D** — *Meiosis*
> **Topic:** `Types of Cell Division`
> **Explanation:** Meiosis creates cells with half the number of chromosomes (haploid) as the original cell and occurs during gamete production.

### Question 18
**What are the two major phases that comprise the eukaryotic cell cycle?**

- **a.)** Prophase and telophase
- **b.)** Mitosis and meiosis
- **c.)** Interphase and cell division
- **d.)** G1 and G2

> **Answer:** **C** — *Interphase and cell division*
> **Topic:** `Cell Cycle`
> **Explanation:** The cell cycle consists of two major phases: interphase and cell division (mitosis + cytokinesis).

### Question 19
**The period between cell divisions during which the cell grows and replicates its DNA and organelles is called what?**

- **a.)** Anaphase
- **b.)** Cytokinesis
- **c.)** Interphase
- **d.)** Metaphase

> **Answer:** **C** — *Interphase*
> **Topic:** `Cell Cycle`
> **Explanation:** Interphase is the period between cell divisions during which DNA and organelles replicate in preparation for division.

### Question 20
**What are the three sequential sub-phases of interphase?**

- **a.)** G1, S, and G2
- **b.)** G0, G1, and G2
- **c.)** Mitosis, meiosis, and cytokinesis
- **d.)** Prophase, metaphase, and anaphase

> **Answer:** **A** — *G1, S, and G2*
> **Topic:** `Interphase`
> **Explanation:** Interphase is divided into G1 (First Gap), S (Synthesis), and G2 (Second Gap) phases.

### Question 21
**During which sub-phase of interphase does major cell growth occur before DNA synthesis begins?**

- **a.)** S phase
- **b.)** G1 phase
- **c.)** G2 phase
- **d.)** M phase

> **Answer:** **B** — *G1 phase*
> **Topic:** `Interphase`
> **Explanation:** G1 (First Gap) is the stage where major cell growth occurs before DNA synthesis begins.

### Question 22
**During which specific sub-phase of interphase does DNA synthesis (replication) take place?**

- **a.)** G2 phase
- **b.)** Prophase
- **c.)** G1 phase
- **d.)** S phase

> **Answer:** **D** — *S phase*
> **Topic:** `Interphase`
> **Explanation:** During the S phase (Synthesis), DNA replication occurs, producing identical copies of genetic material.

### Question 23
**During which sub-phase of interphase does the cell undergo a second stage of growth in final preparation for mitosis?**

- **a.)** G2 phase
- **b.)** Cytokinesis
- **c.)** G1 phase
- **d.)** S phase

> **Answer:** **A** — *G2 phase*
> **Topic:** `Interphase`
> **Explanation:** In the G2 phase, another stage of growth and protein synthesis occurs before mitosis begins.

### Question 24
**In which phase of mitosis does chromatin condense into visible chromosomes, the nuclear membrane break down, and centrioles move to opposite poles?**

- **a.)** Telophase
- **b.)** Anaphase
- **c.)** Prophase
- **d.)** Metaphase

> **Answer:** **C** — *Prophase*
> **Topic:** `Mitosis Phases`
> **Explanation:** During prophase, chromatin condenses, the nuclear membrane breaks down, and centrioles migrate toward opposite ends of the cell.

### Question 25
**Why is the condensation of DNA around histones during prophase critical for cell division?**

- **a.)** It makes it easier to separate sister chromatids without breaking them
- **b.)** It dissolves the cell membrane
- **c.)** It synthesizes new spindle fibers
- **d.)** It speeds up DNA replication

> **Answer:** **A** — *It makes it easier to separate sister chromatids without breaking them*
> **Topic:** `Mitosis Phases`
> **Explanation:** DNA wrapping tightly around histones prevents long DNA strands from tangling and breaking during chromatid separation.

### Question 26
**During which phase of mitosis do chromosomes attach to spindle fibers and line up along the equator (center) of the mitotic spindle?**

- **a.)** Prophase
- **b.)** Metaphase
- **c.)** Telophase
- **d.)** Anaphase

> **Answer:** **B** — *Metaphase*
> **Topic:** `Mitosis Phases`
> **Explanation:** During metaphase, chromosomes attach to spindle fibers and migrate to the equator (metaphase plate) of the mitotic spindle.

### Question 27
**During which phase of mitosis do sister chromatids split at the centromere and get pulled toward opposite poles of the cell?**

- **a.)** Metaphase
- **b.)** Telophase
- **c.)** Prophase
- **d.)** Anaphase

> **Answer:** **D** — *Anaphase*
> **Topic:** `Mitosis Phases`
> **Explanation:** During anaphase, sister chromatids separate at the centromere and are pulled toward opposite cell poles.

### Question 28
**What specialized chromosomal region holds sister chromatids together until it splits during anaphase?**

- **a.)** Centrosome
- **b.)** Telomere
- **c.)** Centriole
- **d.)** Centromere

> **Answer:** **D** — *Centromere*
> **Topic:** `Mitosis Phases`
> **Explanation:** Chromosomes split at the centromere during anaphase as spindle fibers pull them apart.

### Question 29
**In which phase of mitosis does a nuclear envelope reform around each group of chromosomes, the spindle disassemble, and chromosomes decondense?**

- **a.)** Anaphase
- **b.)** Telophase
- **c.)** Prophase
- **d.)** Metaphase

> **Answer:** **B** — *Telophase*
> **Topic:** `Mitosis Phases`
> **Explanation:** During telophase, nuclear envelopes reform, the mitotic spindle disassembles, and chromosomes decondense back into chromatin.

### Question 30
**What is the separate cellular process involving the physical division of the cytoplasm into two daughter cells?**

- **a.)** Cytokinesis
- **b.)** Karyokinesis
- **c.)** Interphase
- **d.)** Apoptosis

> **Answer:** **A** — *Cytokinesis*
> **Topic:** `Cytokinesis`
> **Explanation:** Cytokinesis is the process involving the division of cytoplasm, beginning during telophase.

### Question 31
**At what stage of mitosis does cytokinesis usually begin in human cells?**

- **a.)** Metaphase
- **b.)** Prophase
- **c.)** Telophase
- **d.)** G1 phase

> **Answer:** **C** — *Telophase*
> **Topic:** `Cytokinesis`
> **Explanation:** Cytokinesis usually begins sometime during telophase.

### Question 32
**What cytoskeletal structure contracts at the midline of a dividing animal cell to create a cleavage furrow during cytokinesis?**

- **a.)** Microtubules
- **b.)** Histone octamer
- **c.)** Centrioles
- **d.)** A band of microfilaments

> **Answer:** **D** — *A band of microfilaments*
> **Topic:** `Cytokinesis`
> **Explanation:** A band of microfilaments located at the cell midline contracts, forming a furrow that pinches the cell in two.

### Question 33
**What groove or indentation forms at the cell equator as contracting microfilaments deepen to pinch the cell into two?**

- **a.)** Cleavage furrow
- **b.)** Centromere
- **c.)** Equatorial plate
- **d.)** Cell plate

> **Answer:** **A** — *Cleavage furrow*
> **Topic:** `Cytokinesis`
> **Explanation:** The contracting microfilament band forms a furrow that pinches the cell into two daughter cells once it deepens.

### Question 36
**Which cellular organelle pairs migrate away from each other toward opposite poles during prophase to organize the spindle apparatus?**

- **a.)** Golgi bodies
- **b.)** Lysosomes
- **c.)** Ribosomes
- **d.)** Centrioles

> **Answer:** **D** — *Centrioles*
> **Topic:** `Mitosis Phases`

### Question 37
**What structure is composed of microtubules that attach to chromosomes and direct their alignment and separation during mitosis?**

- **a.)** Cell cortex
- **b.)** Mitotic spindle
- **c.)** Cleavage furrow
- **d.)** Nuclear envelope

> **Answer:** **B** — *Mitotic spindle*
> **Topic:** `Mitosis Phases`
> **Explanation:** The mitotic spindle is composed of spindle fibers that attach to chromosomes at metaphase and pull them apart at anaphase.

### Question 38
**What happens to the mitotic spindle during telophase?**

- **a.)** It disassembles and disappears
- **b.)** It pulls chromatids to the center
- **c.)** It duplicates
- **d.)** It forms the cell membrane

> **Answer:** **A** — *It disassembles and disappears*
> **Topic:** `Mitosis Phases`
> **Explanation:** During telophase, the mitotic spindle that caused chromosomes to migrate disassembles and disappears.

### Question 39
**In which stage of the cell cycle does a cell spend the majority of its functional life performing daily metabolic activities and preparing for division?**

- **a.)** Interphase
- **b.)** Cytokinesis
- **c.)** Mitosis
- **d.)** Anaphase

> **Answer:** **A** — *Interphase*
> **Topic:** `Cell Cycle`
> **Explanation:** Interphase is the longest phase of the cell cycle, consisting of G1, S, and G2 phases.

### Question 40
**What is the primary biological outcome of mitosis followed by cytokinesis?**

- **a.)** A single tetraploid cell
- **b.)** Four genetically diverse haploid gametes
- **c.)** Two non-identical haploid cells
- **d.)** Two genetically identical diploid daughter cells

> **Answer:** **D** — *Two genetically identical diploid daughter cells*
> **Topic:** `Types of Cell Division`
> **Explanation:** Mitosis distributes replicated genetic material equally between two daughter cells, producing identical diploid cells.
